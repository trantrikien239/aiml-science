Getting started

From install to your first ranked list in a few minutes.

  1. Install Predit
  2. Prepare your sheet
  3. Run your first prediction
  4. Read the results
  5. Run it again, or undo
  6. Good to know

1. Install Predit

  1. Open a spreadsheet in Google Sheets™ on a computer.
  2. Click Extensions → Add-ons → Get add-ons and search for Predit, or open the Predit Marketplace page.
  3. Click Install and review the two permissions Google shows you. They are explained in the Privacy Policy.

Predit is free and needs no account. It never opens on its own: you start it from the Extensions menu when you need it.

2. Prepare your sheet

Predit works on one table per sheet tab:

  • A header row with the column names. It is usually row 1; a title above it is fine.
  • One row per record, for example one lead, deal or customer.
  • A column with the answer you want to predict, such as Converted (Yes/No) or Churned (TRUE/FALSE). Fill it in where you know the answer and leave it blank where you want a prediction. "N/A", "-", "?", "TBD", "Pending", "Open" and similar count as not answered yet.
  • Other columns describe each row: numbers, categories, dates. These are what Predit learns from.

You need at least 20 rows with a known answer, including at least 5 of the rarer answer. A few hundred make the trust check much more reliable. The answer column can have up to 10 different answers.

Only use columns you'd know before the outcome. A field that is filled in only after a deal closes "predicts" the past perfectly and the future not at all. Predit spots most such columns and leaves them out, but you know your data best.

3. Run your first prediction

  1. Click Extensions → Predit → Start. The sidebar opens on the right.
  2. Choose the column to predict. Predit suggests the likeliest one and shows how many rows have an answer and how many it will fill in. Under the button it says where the results will go.
  3. Click Predict. The first time, Predit downloads its model (about 85 MB, once) with a progress bar. Then it checks itself on your known rows, predicts the blank ones and writes the results. You can cancel at any time before it writes.
  4. If the check says Not reliable or Too good to be true, Predit writes nothing yet and asks: Write anyway or Don't write.

4. Read the results

Predit adds its columns to the right of your data and fills them only on rows where your answer column is blank. Your own cells are never changed.

ColumnWhat it means
Predit: Converted chanceHow likely the row is to get the answer you care about (for a Yes/No column, Yes). Shown as a percentage when Predit's check shows its percentages hold up on enough rows, otherwise as High, Medium or Low.
Predit: Converted rank1 is the row most likely to get that answer. Sort or filter by it to work down the list.
Predit: Stage (only for columns with 3 or more answers)The most likely answer for the row. The chance column then shows how likely a chosen answer is, or how confident Predit is in its prediction.

In the sidebar you see:

  • The trust score: one label and one sentence on how well Predit did on rows whose answer you already know. See what the trust score means.
  • The most likely rows; click one to jump to it in the sheet.
  • What Predit used and skipped: the columns it learned from and the ones it left out, each with a reason (see why a column was excluded).

The first header cell of Predit's columns carries a note with the date, the trust label and the number of rows used.

5. Run it again, or undo

When new rows arrive or more answers are filled in, open Predit and click Predict again. Predit updates its own columns and leaves everything else alone. Right after a run that added new columns, Undo removes them (if you typed anything else into them, Predit only clears what it wrote). You can also delete the columns whose header starts with "Predit:" at any time.

6. Good to know

  • No limits. Predit is free. It predicts every blank row, however many there are.
  • Big sheets. On a computer with WebGPU, Predit learns from up to 2,000 rows with an answer (somewhat fewer without WebGPU), choosing a balanced sample when you have more.
  • Speed. Predit is fastest in Chrome or Edge with WebGPU. Without it Predit still works, more slowly. See speed tips.
  • Columns. Up to 100 columns are used after exclusions.
  • Privacy. The model runs in your browser; your sheet data is never sent to us. See the Privacy Policy.
