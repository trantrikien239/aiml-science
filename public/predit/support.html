Frequently asked questions

Predit is slow, or says it's running without WebGPU. Why?

Predit runs the model on your computer. It uses your graphics processor through WebGPU when your browser offers it, and falls back to your main processor (WebAssembly) when it doesn't. Both give the same kind of results; the fallback is just slower.

WebGPU is available in current Chrome and Edge on Windows, macOS and ChromeOS. It may be missing on Linux, on older Macs, in some company-managed browsers, or when graphics drivers are out of date. To speed things up:

  • use the latest Chrome or Edge, and update your graphics drivers;
  • close other heavy tabs while Predit runs;
  • on big sheets Predit learns from a representative sample of rows with an answer: up to 2,000 with WebGPU and up to 1,500 without it. It uses fewer on sheets with many columns (on WebGPU, roughly above a dozen used columns). On WebGPU it also learns from about 1,800 rows when there are many rows to predict: from roughly 1,300 blank rows on a 10-column sheet, and about half that at 12 columns. It still predicts every blank row. That is expected and keeps big sheets fast on any computer.
Why does the first run download files, and how big are they?

The first time you run Predit, your browser downloads the prediction model and the engine that runs it: about 85 MB in total, once. A progress bar shows how far it has got. The model comes from huggingface.co and the engine from cdn.jsdelivr.net. These downloads contain no data about you; your sheet is never uploaded.

After that, the files are kept in your browser's cache, so later runs start quickly, in any spreadsheet. The download can happen again if you clear your browser data, use a private window, use a different browser or computer, or after a Predit update changes the model.

If your computer is very low on disk space, Predit keeps the files in memory for the session instead and downloads them again next time.

Why was one of my columns excluded?

Before predicting, Predit checks every column and leaves some out so the results stay honest. The sidebar lists each excluded column with its reason under Details → Skipped, and an amber line names any column it left out because it gives the answer away. The reasons are:

  • Same value everywhere: a column with one value can't help.
  • Looks like an ID: a different value on almost every row, such as IDs, emails or row numbers. These let the model memorise rows instead of learning patterns.
  • Free text: long notes or too many different values to use as categories.
  • Gives the answer away: a column that predicts the answer almost perfectly, or one that is only filled in once the answer is known (for example a "Close reason" filled in only for finished deals). Using it would make the check look much better than real predictions will be.
  • Calculated from the answer: a formula that refers to the column you're predicting.

If you think a column was excluded by mistake, check whether it is really known before the outcome. If it is and Predit still leaves it out, tell us: we use these reports to tune the checks.

What does the trust score mean?

Before writing anything, Predit hides the answer on some rows where you already know it, predicts them from the rest, and compares. It repeats this so every known row is tested once. The result is one label and one sentence, for example: "Tested on 240 past rows: comparing a Yes with a No, it ranked the Yes higher 76 times in 100." 50 in 100 would be no better than a coin toss; 100 in 100 would be perfect.

  • Strong: reliable ranking on enough data. Use the rank and the chance with confidence.
  • Useful: clearly better than guessing. Good for prioritising.
  • Weak: only slightly better than guessing. Use the rank, not the yes/no.
  • Not reliable: no better than always guessing the most common answer. Don't act on these predictions.
  • Too good to be true: some column gave the answer away. Predit leaves it out and checks again.
  • Not tested: fewer than 20 rows with an answer, or fewer than 5 of the rarer answer, so there is no way yet to tell how good the predictions are. Fill in more rows.

With fewer than 100 known rows the label is capped at Useful, because a small check has a wide margin of error. Chances are shown as High/Medium/Low unless the check shows they are well calibrated on enough rows.

How do I uninstall Predit?
  1. Open any spreadsheet in Google Sheets.
  2. Click Extensions → Add-ons → Manage add-ons.
  3. Next to Predit, click Options (the three dots), then Uninstall.

To also remove Predit's permission from your Google Account, follow Google's guide to managing third-party connections. The columns Predit wrote stay in your sheets until you delete them.

Does my sheet data leave my computer?

No. Your sheet data is read by Predit's Apps Script code on Google's servers (that is how every Sheets add-on reads cells) and processed by the model in your browser. It is never sent to us or to anyone else, and Predit doesn't ask for your email address. Details: where your data goes and the Privacy Policy.

The sidebar stays blank, or a button does nothing.

Google Sheets add-ons can misbehave when you're signed in to several Google accounts in the same browser window. Open the sheet in a window (or browser profile) where only one Google account is signed in, then try again.

I can't install Predit with my work account.

Your Google Workspace administrator may only allow approved Marketplace apps. Ask them to allow Predit in the Google Admin console; they can find it on the Predit Marketplace page.
